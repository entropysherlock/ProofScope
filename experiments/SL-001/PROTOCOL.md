# SL-001 protocol (frozen)

**Title.** SL-001: audit of the links between Formal Conjectures statements and the OpenAI Lean proofs
cited as proving them (Erdős problems).

**Version 1.0, frozen 2026-11-11** in the commit that first contains this file, before any Stage 1 or
Stage 2 output for the full case list existed. Disclosure of what was inspected beforehand:
SOURCE_AUDIT.md section 7. Amendments are appended at the end with date, reason and whether results had
been seen; the original text is never edited.

## 1. Research question

For each theorem in Formal Conjectures that is asserted to be formally proved by a declaration in
`openai/math`: (Q1) what exactly are the two statements, when was each written, and is B independent of
A? (Q2) does the OpenAI library, on its own pinned dependencies, yield a kernel-checked proof of B
*verbatim*? Q1 is Stage 1. Q2 is Stage 2.

The original title ("independent audit against independently maintained formalizations") is narrowed:
SOURCE_AUDIT.md section 5 shows independence must be measured per case, not assumed.

## 2. Cases and inclusion

All theorems under `FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/` at `FC_HEAD` whose `category` attribute carries a
`formal_proof` link to `github.com/openai/math`. 18 cases, listed in `cases.jsonl`, generated by
`sl001.py build-cases` and re-checked against the source by `sl001.py verify-freeze`. No case may be
added or removed. A is the linked OpenAI declaration; B is the Formal Conjectures theorem statement.

## 5. Axioms and assumptions

Allowed: `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. Anything else, `sorryAx` included, rejects the
certificate. A `conditional formal_proof` link is reported separately and never counted as unconditional.

## 6. Stage 1 (deterministic observations)

Per case, record: the B statement at `FC_PRE`, `FC_LINK`, `FC_HEAD` (text and hash; an `answer(...) ↔`
wrapper is stripped only for the purpose of comparing bodies, and the stripping is flagged); the date and
author of the B file's first commit; the A declaration name and statement at `OAI_LINKED` and `OAI_HEAD`;
whether A's statement changed; OpenAI's Comparator config for A, if any, with its permitted axioms; the
`def`s that are verbatim identical between A's challenge file and B's pre-release file; the size of A's
import closure.

**Independence class (frozen rule, first match wins).**
1. `B_written_after_release`: the theorem does not exist at `FC_PRE`.
2. `B_edited_when_linked`: the statement body differs between `FC_PRE` and `FC_LINK`.
3. `B_predates_release_but_A_shares_verbatim_definitions`.
4. `B_predates_release_unchanged_no_verbatim_overlap_found`.

Class 4 is the only class that is *compatible with* independence; it does not prove it.
Stage 1 outcomes are `observed`, `unsupported_input` or `blocked_infrastructure`. Stage 1 certifies nothing.

## 7. Stage 2 (kernel bridge) — frozen, not launched in v1.0

**Transport.** B's statement at `FC_HEAD` and the definitions it uses are copied verbatim into A's
environment. Permitted edits: replacing the `module` header and imports, removing Formal Conjectures
attributes and `@[expose] public section`. Any other edit makes the case `unsupported_input`. The
transported B must elaborate without error (`compile_failure` otherwise) and its hash is recorded.

**Bridge.** A proof of the transported B that may use the OpenAI library. Track D (deterministic): the
fixed cascade `exact A`, `simpa using A`, `exact_mod_cast A`, `simpa [<definitions of A and B>] using A`,
each ≤ 10 minutes. Track H (authored): a bridge written by a person or a model, same kernel checks,
reported separately. Authorship never affects soundness; it is recorded for effort accounting.

**Certificate requirements.** Elaboration exit code 0 with no error; no `sorry`; proposition re-checked by
elaborating `theorem _ : <B> := @bridge`; axioms within section 5; module replayed by `leanchecker`;
all on pinned dependencies. `#print axioms` alone is never sufficient. OpenAI's own Comparator configs are
run where the tooling is available and reported as a separate column.

**Outcome vocabulary.** `certified` (B established from the library), `undecided` (no bridge found on
elaborated input), `timeout`, `unsupported_input`, `compile_failure`, `blocked_infrastructure`.

**Logic rules.** (a) `certified` means: *the pinned OpenAI library entails B*. It does not mean A and B are
equivalent, and it says nothing about Erdős's intent. (b) A failed bridge search is `undecided`, never
"B is not proved". (c) If A → B is certified and B → A is not, the record reads "one-way implication
established; reverse direction undecided". (d) A strict difference is recorded only with a kernel-checked
separating witness over a stated class of structures. (e) Both sides are closed propositions; if both are
provable they are logically equivalent whatever they say, so no "equivalence" record is issued for closed
statements. Equivalence is only meaningful for schemas with stated allowed steps.

## 8. Competing methods

Stage 1 has no competitor; it is bookkeeping. For Stage 2 the comparison points are (i) the source-level
review recorded in PR #6898, (ii) OpenAI's Comparator configs, (iii) SemantEq and BEq+ on the pairs where
A's challenge statement and B are both open-form statements. ScopeLedger reuses those tools; it does not
reimplement them, and makes no state-of-the-art claim.

## 9. Smoke tests (must pass before any real case is read by the kernel pipeline)

| Test | Input | Required outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a statement against itself | both directions certified |
| 2 | `4 ∣ n` versus `2 ∣ n` on ℕ | forward certified; failed reverse attempt is `compile_failure` and the relation stays "reverse undecided"; with the witness `n = 2`, separation certified; a forward proof offered for the reverse direction is rejected |
| 3 | an error-recovered proof; a proof containing `sorry` | `compile_failure`; `unsupported_input`; never accepted |
| 4 | a valid proof of a weaker schema than the target | does not establish the target; its actual proposition is certified; "target implies proved; reverse undecided" |

## 10. Budgets

Stage 1: 30 minutes total. Smoke: 10 minutes. Stage 2 feasibility probe (one module build, Erdős 970):
300 minutes, reported as infrastructure only. Stage 2 proper: 350 minutes per case job.

## 11. Metrics

Stage 1: counts per independence class; links that resolve; A statements changed after linking; B
statements changed after linking; cases with an OpenAI Comparator config. Stage 2: of 18, the number
`certified` (Track D and Track H separately), and each other outcome. Blocked and unsupported cases are
never folded into "undecided". Independently reviewed: counted only when a human other than the authors
has signed a review record; expected to be 0 in v1.0.

## 12. Decision rules (Stage 2, frozen now)

* **SUCCESS:** at least 9 of 18 `certified`, reproducible from a clean checkout on hosted runners.
* **PIVOT:** 3–8 `certified`, or ≥ 9 only with Track H bridges. Report as a labour-cost finding.
* **STOP:** fewer than 3 `certified`, or the probe shows the library cannot be built per case on free
  infrastructure. Fall back to the corrected-versus-original Formal Conjectures experiment (MisformBench).

Stage 1 has no success threshold; it is reported whatever it shows.

## 13. Threats to validity

Statement extraction is regex-based and may truncate unusual syntax (hashes make this detectable). A's
real proposition may sit behind project-local definitions, so textual comparison of A and B is weak
evidence. B is frequently not independent of A. `FC_HEAD` statements were edited after the release by
maintainers who had seen A. A kernel proof of B from the library says nothing about the informal problem.
All work here was produced by an AI-assisted pipeline directed by one person; none of it is independent
review. The case list covers only links that Formal Conjectures chose to add.
